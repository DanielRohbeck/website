Case study · Mercedes-Benz · 2022 – today

Resilient planning at scale

One scenario-capable plan from sales to supply, built and funded as one pooled portfolio.

01 · Challenge

Planning that could not bend

  • The 2022 chip shortage showed it plainly: every change produced one deterministic plan that cascaded down to suppliers.
  • There was no way to simulate scenarios without committing to them.

Mindset

From deterministic plans to scenarios and probabilities.

Organization

Four divisions planning together instead of handing numbers over.

Systems

An IT landscape never built for scenarios. Shared master data had to come first.

Read the full context

In 2022, the semiconductor shortage exposed what earlier crises (COVID, CO₂ targets, ramp-up studies) had already hinted at: vehicle planning was built for stability, not for scenarios. Every change in assumptions triggered a single deterministic plan that cascaded all the way down to suppliers. There was no way to simulate scenarios without committing to them.

The goal: make the planning chain resilient in two directions. First, be prepared: simulate scenarios deliberately, without feeding them straight into the live plan. Second, react faster: carry scenarios down the planning chain so that changes can be absorbed quickly.

The change had to happen on three levels at once. Mindset: from deterministic plans to thinking in scenarios and probabilities. Organization: Sales, Production, Procurement and Finance each owned their own numbers and handed them over sequentially, and integrated planning meant they had to plan together. Systems: the IT landscape was never built for scenarios, so many systems had to change and shared master data had to be created first.

02 · What I did

Build it, pool it, keep it alive

  • Set up the program from scratch as one cross-functional organization: teams, release trains, method.
  • Pooled the divisions’ budgets into one portfolio, one of the first Lean Portfolio Management setups in the group.
  • Renewed the portfolio’s mandate in every annual budget round, with two levers:

Sponsors and a story that moves

Sponsors in every division. The value story retold whenever the business shifts: from “how do we build cars at all?” in 2022 to “how do we match production to demand?” today.

Ship early, prove value

Everything live as fast as possible. Real users, visible benefits, and a business case for every budget round.

Read the full story

The program was set up from scratch as one cross-functional organization. That meant staffing the teams, building the Agile Release Trains, establishing the method, and bringing IT, Production and Sales into joint teams. Portfolio steering across the planning chain required a new funding model: budgets from the individual divisions were pooled centrally and prioritized as one portfolio. This was one of the first applications of Lean Portfolio Management within the group.

Pooling budgets is not a one-off decision. Priorities are re-agreed every budget cycle, and each year the divisions’ own initiatives and the shared portfolio have to be weighed against each other. Securing alignment on one set of priorities across four divisions became the core of my work.

Sponsors and a story that moves with the business

We built close ties with stakeholders and management in every division and cultivated sponsors who advocated for the program in their own areas. Just as important, the value story had to be retold whenever the business context shifted. In 2022 the question was how to build cars at all when every car would sell. Today it is how to match production to demand. Same capabilities, a different answer to the problem that matters now.

Ship early, prove value

From day one, everything we built went live as fast as possible. That created real users who valued our processes and tools, some of whom came to depend on them. It made benefits visible early. And it gave us a solid business case that we could put on the table in every budget round.

03 · Results

Business case validated by Controlling.

Foundation

Read the full results

End-to-end strategic planning, live

One integrated plan from Sales through Production to Supply, for both cars and vans, fully scenario-capable. The planning cycle has been cut by around half, which frees time for more scenarios and portfolio case studies and gives top management a more resilient basis for decisions.

A shared foundation underneath

The less visible but perhaps most lasting result is one master data basis across all divisions and across cars and vans, a first within the company. A central data hub adds a data layer where we build and maintain the data products we need, and increasingly the ones we deliver to the wider group.

Scenarios further down the chain

The chain is now closed towards short-term planning, with scenarios in place there as well and chained planning starting. We have piloted agentic AI in production planning and, on that basis, connected sales and production planning.

01 · Challenge

Same speed, new target

  • Digital Business Development tested new digital business models with the business units, from monetization to subscription billing.
  • Then the group’s focus moved to cost and efficiency. The question became: can the same speed work on core processes?
Read the full context

I helped build Digital Business Development at Mercedes-Benz Management Consulting, with a team drawn from agencies, start-ups and the group itself. Together with the business units we tested new digital business models, from monetization strategies to subscription billing.

When the group’s focus moved from growth through new digital models to cost and efficiency, I co-developed the idea for the Digital Accelerator in a team of three, and we pitched it internally together. Once it was approved, I moved into the new unit and built it. The mission was to apply the methods and speed we had used for new business models (design thinking, fast prototypes, early live use) to the core processes of the company.

02 · What I did

Into the task forces

  • Co-developed the idea for the Digital Accelerator, pitched it internally as a team of three and built the unit: data scientists, engineers, process experts and consultants.
  • Built its data science and platform half, about 15 people, including a cloud analytics platform.

Production ramp-up

Analyses that became management decisions, such as prioritizing high-margin vehicles.

Ramp-up steering

Transparency dashboards as a shared basis for steering the ramp-up.

COVID

Scenarios for plant ramp-down and ramp-up, built in Python and Excel.

Read the full story

We built a team of data scientists, data engineers, process experts and consultants. We went into projects across the group, analyzed processes and delivered fast first digital solutions. I moved into task-force work early.

Production ramp-up: data science analyses that translated into management decisions, for example prioritizing high-margin vehicles and optimizing logistics chains.

Ramp-up steering: transparency dashboards as a shared basis for coordinating the ramp-up across departments.

COVID: alongside the base plan, scenarios for the course of the pandemic and for optimal ramp-down and ramp-up of the plants, built pragmatically in Python and Excel as an alternative planning layer to steer through the crisis.

At its peak the Accelerator had around 30 people, half of them in my area: data science and the technical platform, including a cloud-based analytics platform and DevOps support. The other half formed the consulting branch. The unit was established as a spin-off of Digital Business Development.

03 · Results

Impact that recurs

  • Recurring EBIT impact in the high double-digit millions within two years, validated by Controlling, driven mainly by margin optimization.
  • From task force to product: a tool that allocates scarce capacity by value contribution, systematically rather than case by case. It later moved into the MB.CO planning landscape.
  • A unit of about 30 people, built from scratch as a spin-off of Digital Business Development.

Led toThe crisis scenarios showed what planning lacked: the ability to think in scenarios. MB.CO built exactly that at scale.

A payment platform built from an accounting team

01 · Challenge

More than a payment system

  • Replace car2go’s payment system, starting from a team that was essentially accounting.
  • Serve car2go and the Mercedes-Benz digital services too. The digital services had no monetization strategies yet and needed business consulting as well as payment.
Read the full context

In 2015 I joined Daimler Mobility Services, the holding behind car2go and mytaxi, as a team lead. The first assignment was to replace car2go’s payment system. The existing payment team was essentially an accounting team, focused on direct-debit processing, collections and receivables management.

Scaling transactions economically required a real payment system, and the ambition grew beyond car2go: payment for the Mercedes-Benz digital services as well. At the time, these services had no monetization strategies. So they needed business consulting as much as payment: before building payment capabilities, we first had to work out the business models with our future internal customers.

02 · What I did

Team, platform, business models

Own platform

Accounting, integrations and payment methods, built from the ground up.

Business models

Micro-transactions and subscriptions, co-developed with internal customers.

US and China

Local payment incl. Alipay and WeChat Pay, with a licensed e-money partner.

Read the full story

I built the team and the platform from the ground up: our own technical platform with accounting, integrations and a range of payment methods. Alongside the technology we did a lot of business consulting, covering micro-transactions, subscription management, customer master data and processes, and we co-developed monetization strategies with the digital products.

Because car2go operated in the US and China, we set up local payment systems there too. China was the earliest and toughest challenge, with Alipay and WeChat Pay integration. We worked with a small Luxembourg start-up holding its own payment license, which the group later acquired. That brought a full e-money setup, including a Luxembourg e-money license, into the structure, so our products could run on it.

At its peak, 17 people covered the full value chain of a payment business: product ownership for the technical platform, payment operations, direct-debit settlement in the accounting system, and collections. We also owned the interfaces to the group’s legal teams and to the Luxembourg partner, which developed the platform further and ran the regulatory side. Internal sales and the onboarding of new digital services onto the platform completed the picture.

03 · Results

Live on three continents

  • Over 2 million transactions per month
  • Live across car2go, mytaxi and group digital services in Europe, the US and China
  • A regulated e-money setup with its own license inside the group

Led toDigital services still lacked monetization strategies. Digital Business Development took that on next.

01 · Challenge

Launch a wallet, find the business

  • Take a white-label mobile payment solution from idea to launch, as founder and CEO.
  • Prove it in a B2B2C pilot in Osnabrück: 2,000 customers, 50+ merchants.

02 · What I did

A decision, then a pivot

Launch

Product built and piloted with customers and merchants in one city.

Decide

We chose to scale without outside investors, and acquisition costs proved too high.

Pivot

The same capabilities as B2B payment services: multi-merchant setups, micropayment billing.

Read the full story

We decided to scale without outside investors and learned the hard way that customer acquisition and marketing costs exceeded what we could carry. To be fair, mobile payment also delivered less business benefit than other business models at the time.

We repositioned the capabilities we had built as a B2B2C payment processing service: multi-merchant platform setups and micropayment billing. We then expanded into B2B, winning energy companies and mobility providers as clients. The competence stayed the same, but without our own consumer platform up front.

This also fitted the group far better: mr.net was a B2B provider of white-label solutions, and an own consumer brand had never been a natural fit. The business grew in e-commerce and in billing for digital services.

03 · Results

Growth where it fit

  • New B2B clients among energy companies and mobility providers
  • Growth in e-commerce and billing for digital services
  • A model that fit the group’s white-label B2B business
